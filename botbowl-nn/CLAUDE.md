# CLAUDE.md — botbowl-nn

Neural-network plumbing for the MCTS bot (plan 017): the **shared feature encoder**, the **offline prepare step**, and the **tract-onnx inference evaluator**. Depends on `botbowl-engine` + `botbowl-data`; **no** dependency on `botbowl-mcts` (mcts depends on nn, not vice versa). The PyTorch trainer lives outside the workspace in `../train/` (a `uv` project).

## The one rule: encoding lives here, in Rust, once

`encode.rs` is the **single source of tensor layout**, used verbatim by both the offline `prepare` bin and the live `NnEvaluator`. Python never parses a `GameState` — it only consumes the `.npy` batches `prepare` writes. This is deliberate: there is **no train/inference encoding skew** possible. If you change a channel, you change it here and the manifest's `spatial_channel_names`/`global_feature_names` update automatically; bump `NN_SCHEMA_VERSION` in `bin/prepare.rs`.

Shapes (schema v9): spatial `C=61` planes × `H` × `W`, global `F=18`, policy head `A=32` (v8 re-laid the policy channels for the per-player setup without changing a shape — `PosAT::PlacePlayer` at 14, the simple block at 15.., `SimpleAT::BenchPlayer` at 29, the formation actions gone — so a checkpoint carries a `schema_version` buffer and `migrate.py` permutes a v7 head, keeping every retained channel's weights; v9 appended `SimpleAT::UseSkill`/`DontUseSkill` at 30/31 for optional skills, zero rows in the migration). `C` is **derived**, not a literal: `2 present + 8 shared per-player + ENCODED_SKILLS + 12 shared`, where `ENCODED_SKILLS` (`encode.rs`) is the prefix of `Skill::ALL` that has planes — skills the engine names but does not implement yet (`StripBall`) are appended to `ALL` past it. Giving one a plane (raising `ENCODED_SKILLS`) widens the tensor and invalidates every checkpoint's *shape* — bump `NN_SCHEMA_VERSION`, mirror the number in `train/src/bbnn/model.py`, rebuild both fixtures (`uv run python -m bbnn.fixture`, and the 14x7 goldens per `tests/capacity_parity.rs`) — **and register one step in `train/src/bbnn/migrate.py`** so trained weights carry over: `python -m bbnn.migrate old.pt --out new.pt --onnx new.onnx` detects the schema from the tensor shapes and applies each registered `Migration` in order, like a chain of commits. Inputs added at the end of a block are inserted as zero columns, so the migrated net computes exactly what the old one did until training moves them; every step is verified by running both nets on random inputs (`test_migrate.py` pins the chain is contiguous and exact). A step that cannot be function-preserving says `exact=False` and carries its own check. **If the champion's `.pt` is gone and only its `.onnx` survives**, `python -m bbnn.from_onnx champ.onnx --out champ.pt` reconstructs a loadable state_dict first: the export folds BatchNorm into the convolutions, so γ/β/μ/σ² cannot come back, but an eval-mode-equivalent net can (BN becomes `y = x + b'`, verified against the ONNX reference runtime). Inference-equivalent, *not* the trainer's original weights — warm-starting from it re-estimates BN statistics. `H`/`W` are the **runtime `board_dims` including the 2-cell OOB border** — a `Position` indexes the tensor directly, and border cells are flagged by the `oob` plane and masked out of the policy (the network never emits masking; masking = "only gather legal actions", done identically in `eval.rs` and `train/model.py`).

**Per-player planes are unpaired; only `present` carries a side (schema v5).** A square holds at most one player (`add_new_player_to_field` rejects an occupied cell), so `us_present`/`them_present` already identify the owner and duplicating the other 47 per-player planes per side was pure redundancy — 103 channels became 56 (58 with the endzone planes below). It is lossless and recoverable in the *stem*: for binary planes `a` with `b = us_present`, `ReLU(a + b − 1.5)` is "us has `a`" and `ReLU(a − b − 0.5)` is "them has `a`"; for a characteristic `v`, `ReLU(v + b − 1)` is the mover's `v`. **That last identity needs `v ≤ 1`**, which is why the per-player normalisers are the engine's `PlayerStats::MAX_*` caps and not round numbers — `MOVE_NORM` was 10 against a real maximum of `MA + 2 = 11`, so movement_left encoded to 1.1 and the recovery silently stopped being exact. `per_player_planes_are_normalised_into_the_unit_interval` and `sampling_ranges_stay_under_the_engine_caps` are what hold that. The tackle-zone planes stay paired: they are neighbour counts, not attached to the player in the cell, and both teams can cover one square.

**`path_prob` is recomputed, never read from `state.path_buffer`.** The plane is the active player's per-square probability of arriving (the move's cumulative chance across every dodge, GFI and pickup), which the engine has already computed and cached — but that cache is `#[serde(skip)]` **and** `#[derivative(PartialEq = "ignore")]`. A state that has been through the corpus comes back with `has_paths == true` and an *empty* buffer, so an encoder that read it would emit an all-zero plane in training and a populated one at inference: silent skew, with the loss converging happily the whole time. Ignoring it in `PartialEq` is the second, independent problem — two states that recombine to one DAG node may hold different buffers, which would make the NN prior impure and break recombination. So the encoder calls `PathFinder::player_paths`, a pure function of `(state, id)`, and `recomputed_paths_match_the_engines_own_buffer` pins that it agrees with the engine's own buffer square for square. The `has_paths` gate *is* serialised and *is* in `PartialEq`, so gating on it stays pure. **This costs real time** — `encode` is ~3 µs without paths and ~50 µs with them (plan 038) — and **the cache is not a shortcut, even when it is populated**: at a `BlockAction` decision `has_paths` is true and the buffer holds direct-block offerings, not the mover's movement paths, so "read the buffer when present" would encode block decisions differently at inference than in the corpus. `the_engines_buffer_is_not_the_path_plane_at_a_block_decision` is the counter-example; leave it in. The cost is instead paid once per node, not twice, by the evaluation memo below.

It is also the one **lossy** plane: the pathfinder's `f32` is quantised to 1/255, floored at 1 so an unlikely path never reads as unreachable (`0` means exactly "no path"). `encode_raw` stores the quantised value and `encode` divides by 255, so the raw/normalised identity still holds bit-for-bit — the loss is at the input, not between the two views.

**Geometry is an input, not something to infer from the padding (schema v7, plan 042).** Two canonical-frame planes, `dist_to_us_endzone` (`x − 1`) and `dist_to_sideline` (`min(y − 1, h − 2 − y)`), and three globals, `playable_w / 26`, `playable_h / 15`, `team_size / 11`. Without them a translation-equivariant tower on one fixed board learns absolute position from its distance to the zero-padded edge, which is exactly what mixed-size training has to take away — and a pooled value head has no other way to know how long the pitch is. Absolute coordinate planes were rejected as the memorisable form. `geometry_planes_and_size_globals_follow_the_runtime_board` pins both under either mover.

**`us_td_zone` / `them_td_zone` are the only thing telling the net which way it attacks.** The tower is translation-equivariant and `oob` is symmetric in `x` (`is_out` is `x <= 0 || x >= width - 1`), so before these planes existed *nothing* in the tensor distinguished the scoring end from the conceding one — canonicalisation fixes the convention but cannot communicate it, and the policy head had no basis to prefer a move toward `x = 1` over the identical move toward `x = w - 2`. They are read from `dims.endzone_x` rather than hardcoded to the canonical column, so they cannot drift from the rules; `td_zones_are_canonical_for_either_mover` asserts they land on `x = 1` / `x = w - 2` under **both** movers, which doubles as an independent check on the `Away` x-mirror.

## Perspective is a single authority (`perspective.rs`)

`mover_for(state)` = `available_actions.team.unwrap_or(team_turn)`. Everything perspective-dependent — the encoder, the action↔cell map, the value sign — routes through here. **Canonical view: the mover attacks toward `x=1`.** `Home` already does (`endzone_x(Home)==1`) → encoded verbatim; `Away` is **x-mirrored** (`x → (width-1)-x`, no y-flip; involutive). The network's value `v` is **mover-centric** (`+1` = team-to-move winning); Home-centric is recovered by flipping sign for an `Away` mover.

The game's *other* mirror — across the pitch's long axis, `y → (H-1)-y` — IS a true symmetry of the *engine* (left/right of the attacking direction are interchangeable) and is exploited as **train-time random y-flip augmentation** in `train/src/bbnn/data.py` (`flip_y`; positional action cells flip y, simple/channel-max logits and value are invariant). Caveat: the *search's* collapsed chance model is not y-symmetric — every scripted bounce/scatter/deviate goes `Direction::up()` (`botbowl-mcts/src/roll_outcomes.rs`) — so policy targets near a sideline with a loose ball carry a small y-asymmetry the augmentation flips. Value targets come from real dice and are unaffected. Measured small (plan 023 y-control); plan 031 D7. The x-mirror is *not* a symmetry — it swaps attacking directions and is already spent on the canonicalisation above.

## Action ↔ policy cell (`actions.rs`)

Exhaustive matches pin `PosAT → 0..15`, `SimpleAT → 15..30`. Adding an engine action variant is a **compile error here** — a deliberate trip-wire forcing a schema bump. `v8_channel_layout_matches_the_migration` pins the layout `train/src/bbnn/migrate.py`'s `POLICY_MAP_7_TO_8` assumes. Positional actions target one canonicalised cell `(channel, y, x)`; simple actions have no cell and their logit is the channel-wide spatial **max**.

## Targets (`targets.rs`) — never train π on raw visit counts

A `Sample::scripted` decision (a formation-driven setup placement, or a forced single choice — plan 047) is one-hot on the action played whatever the target kind, and `prepare` keeps a formation sample despite its `root_visits = 1` (a forced one has a single child and is skipped, see prepare below). Otherwise: `recon_mcts` freezes a solved child's visits, and the fastest-solving child is often the *best* move, so `π ∝ visits` is wrong (plan 017 §caveat). `policy_target`: root solved → one-hot on argmax mover-`Q` (or `Skip`); partially solved → visits, but the argmax-`Q` solved child is floored at the max unsolved-sibling visit count; else normalise visits. All `Q` comparisons are in the **mover's** frame (`Away` negates the Home-centric stored `Q`). `value_target` = mover-signed outcome `z`. `policy_target_of(.., PolicyTargetKind::CompletedQ { tau })` (plan 032 #7; `prepare --policy-target cq --tau 100`) is the Gumbel-style alternative for unsolved roots: `softmax(ln prior + q_mover/τ)`, children with no `Q` or zero visits completed by the visit-weighted mean `Q` of the visited ones; solved roots stay one-hot. The prior's global scale cancels in the softmax, so heuristic and NN corpora both work. `PolicyTargetKind::GumbelQ { c_visit, c_scale, min_range }` (`prepare --policy-target gumbel`, plan 049 finding 1) is Gumbel MuZero's form: `softmax(ln prior + (c_visit + maxN)·c_scale·q̂)` with `q̂` the same completed mover-`Q` min-max-normalised over the root's children (floored at `min_range` Q points), so the sharpness follows each root's own spread instead of a fixed `τ`; both share `completed_mover_q` and `softmax_over_prior`, so the two cannot drift apart on completion. `scripts/audit_q_target.py` holds the reference Python implementation (agrees to 3e-8) and scores any target against the 16k convergence reference.

## prepare (`bin/prepare.rs`)

`prepare --in *.jsonl --out DIR [--solved-root onehot|skip] [--min-root-visits N] [--policy-target visits|cq --tau T] [--keep-forced]`. Streams `DatasetReader`, groups samples **by board dims** (one `dims_{w}x{h}` subdir each → fixed-shape batches for free; since plan 042 the trainer's `open_prepared(DIR)` reads *all* of them with a per-board batch sampler — never `head -1` a `dims_*` listing), writes `spatial/global/value/chosen` `.npy` + a **CSR** ragged legal-action encoding (`actions.npy (M,4)`, `policy.npy (M,)`, `action_offsets.npy (N+1,)`) + `manifest.json`. `npy.rs` is a hand-rolled `.npy` v1.0 writer/reader (avoids the ndarray stack).

**Forced decisions are never trained on.** A sample whose recorded root had fewer than two children — `children` is the root's post-pruning action set, so this is a decision with one action left, which `MctsBot` plays unsearched and records as a one-child scripted sample — is dropped before the target filters and counted as `with fewer than two actions` in the summary; the manifest says `skip_forced`. The per-drive value weight (W4, `1 / kept rows`) counts only the rows kept. `--keep-forced` restores the old behaviour. The corpus still holds those samples: replay (`override_audit`, `mc_label`) needs every `chosen_action`. `prepare_skips_samples_with_fewer_than_two_children` pins it.

**Peak RSS is O(1) in corpus size, and must stay that way.** `spatial` is 8,784 bytes/sample at C=61 (8,496 at C=59; it was 21,312 at C=37/f32: the u8 corpus and the unpaired per-player planes together more than paid back the 6→39 skill widening); buffering it cost 2.03 GB of RSS for one generation and OOM-killed `prepare` at a 3-generation window on the 14.4 GB training box. The corpus-sized arrays now go straight to disk through `npy::StreamWriter`, which reserves `STREAM_HEADER_RESERVE` (128) bytes, streams rows, then seeks back and back-patches the header once `N` is known — legal because a v1.0 header's length depends only on dtype+shape, and byte-identical to the buffered writer for every shape `prepare` emits (all of them pad to 128; `finish` asserts if one ever wouldn't fit). A two-pass "count then write" was rejected: parsing is most of the runtime. Only `value`/`chosen`/`offsets` stay in RAM (~20 B/sample). Measured on gen00's 6 train shards (99,059 samples, at C=37/f32): 2,134,156 kB → 8,984 kB peak, output md5-identical.

**`spatial.npy` is `u8` raw counts, not pre-normalised `f32` (schema v4).** Every spatial channel is an integer quantity — a flag, a characteristic, a tackle-zone count — so `u8` is *exact*, not a quantisation, and `encode_raw` is the source of truth with `encode` defined as `encode_raw / spatial_channel_scales()`. That identity is pinned bit-for-bit by `raw_u8_planes_reproduce_the_normalised_ones_exactly`. The scales ride in the manifest as `spatial_scales` and `train/src/bbnn/data.py` divides by them; **never hardcode them in Python** — that is the one place train/inference skew can still get in, which is why a manifest without them is a hard error rather than a default. The live path (`eval.rs`, `remote.rs`) is untouched and still f32: the win is disk and dataloader, not inference. Quantising the *socket* payload was considered and rejected — `movement_left/10` is not u8-representable, so it would break the `< 1e-3` tract-parity canary.

## eval (`eval.rs`) — frozen NN, pure function of state

`NnEvaluator::from_path` loads ONNX; the model has dynamic `H`/`W`, so we build one tract runnable **per `(H,W)`** on first use and cache it (board dims are constant per game → one entry). `priors(state, &actions)` = one forward, gather per-legal-action logits (positional cell / simple channel-max), softmax in Rust, **rescale ×`len`** so mean prior ≈ 1.0. `value_home_i64(state)` = mover-centric `v`, clamped to `[-1,1]`, sign-flipped Home-centric, **×1000** (matches `leaf_score`'s TD = ±1000). A frozen deterministic CPU net is a **pure function of state** → recombination-safe. The ×1000 and ×K rescales are calibration bridges coupled to `PUCT_C`/`leaf_score` in `botbowl-mcts`.

**One encode and one forward per node (`LAST_FORWARD`).** The search scores a node, then asks for its priors, on the same state and thread; a thread-local slot serves the second call. It is **keyed on the `GameState` under `PartialEq`** — the identity `StoreState` recombination uses, and stricter than the encoded tensor since the encoder reads nothing `PartialEq` ignores — **and on the evaluator** (`NnEvaluator::id`, process-unique; plan 068). Before that a second net asking for the same state on the same thread was served the first net's forward: an eval drive's candidate and opponent alternate on one thread, so the opponent's first root forward could be the candidate's. Never a hash (the `HashOnly` lesson), and no longer the tensor: comparing tensors meant running the encoder, pathfinder included, just to build the key. `tests/memo.rs` pins one forward per node and hit ≡ fresh evaluation; `examples/encode_bench.rs` is the microbenchmark. Measured ~70–100 µs less non-NN time per forward, ~10% end-to-end on the sidecar (plan 038 §Open items).

## remote (`remote.rs`) — the batched sidecar backend (plan 024)

`NnEvaluator` has two backends. **tract is the default and the numerics reference**; `Backend::Remote` forwards to `scripts/nn_server.py` over a Unix socket and **always carries a tract fallback**, so an absent/dead/wedged server makes a shard *slow*, never broken (one `NN_SERVER_FALLBACK` warning, count printed at exit). Turn it on with `--nn-server PATH` on `dataset`/`eval`, or `BLOOD_NN_SERVER`; unset ⇒ byte-identical to before, no GPU, no new Cargo dependency, works on a Mac.

- `forward_raw` (+ the value-only sibling) is the **single dispatch point** — `priors`, `value_home_i64` and both `botbowl-mcts/src/dynamics.rs` call sites are untouched and nothing is async. `value_home_i64` sets `want_policy = false`, cutting the response from 17 KB to 4 B (that's the generator's whole traffic).
- **The canary handshake is a safety interlock, not a smoke test.** A connection is bound to one model; the server returns *that model's* result on the committed `parity_9x16_*` fixture and the client compares to its own tract answer at `< 1e-3`, aborting the process on mismatch. Serving the wrong weights is otherwise undetectable — it produces a plausible corpus, or a plausible promotion-gate verdict, labelled with the wrong net. The `model_id` echo check on every request is the second, independent guard.
- **Connections are pooled per evaluator** (`RemoteClient.idle`): a forward checks one out, exchanges, returns it. The pool grows to the peak number of concurrent forwards, so parallel games still reach the server as independent batchable streams, and the eval phase's two nets never share a connection. It replaced a `thread_local!` map that cost **one handshake per decision**, because `MctsBot` runs each search on a fresh scoped thread: 69.5k connections for 69.8k decisions in gen10 (plan 046), each re-resolving the model inside the server's single-threaded loop. `short_lived_threads_reuse_a_pooled_connection` pins it.
- Numerics: torch-CUDA vs tract agree to ~1e-5 in practice; the contract is `< 1e-3` for remote-vs-tract and `< 1e-5` for batch invariance (`tests/remote.rs`). `tests/parity.rs` keeps its 1e-4 tract-vs-PyTorch assertion **unchanged** — tract is still the reference.
- `BLOOD_NN_PROFILE=1` prints `NN_PROFILE forwards=… share=…` per game and at exit: the forward counter that measured the 85% inference share behind plan 024.
- **Model identity, server-side, is the resolved `.pt` file — never the client's path string.** Two spellings of one net (`models/x.onnx` vs an absolute path) must share a `model_id` and a batch queue; keying on the string rejects the second spelling at `--max-models 1`, or loads the net twice and halves the batch above 1. Both fail silently, as lost speed. Pinned by `live_server_identifies_a_model_by_its_weights_not_its_path_string`.
- **`--canvas HxW` puts every board size in one batch queue (plan 046).** Without it the server batches per `(model, h, w)`, and the size curriculum spread 10 streams over 12 shapes: mean batch 1.55, GPU 95% busy on launches, not arithmetic. With it, each request that fits is staged top-left on the canvas with a mask, and `BBNet.forward_masked` re-zeroes the padding after every layer and means the value head over the real cells only. That is exactly the unpadded result up to float reassociation (`train/tests/test_model.py`, every curriculum board, including mixed batches), and the policy row is cropped back to the client's `h × w`. `train_loop.sh` passes the build capacity (`NN_CANVAS`, `off` to disable). Keep `forward_masked` in step with `forward`.
- **Batches are padded up to a CUDA-graph bucket, which is sound only because BatchNorm is in `eval` mode** — rows are independent, so a padding row cannot reach a real one. `live_server_is_batch_invariant` is what pins that, and it is the test to check first if anyone ever touches `.eval()` or the graph pool. It compares `max |v − reference|`; an earlier version compared `max(0.0, v)` and was vacuous for any net with negative values.
- Throughput: `--nn-server --parallel-games 4` measured **3.79× end-to-end** against tract, at **4.3× less CPU per forward** (plan 024 §Measured results, stages 3–5). tract is internally multi-threaded and was the CPU hog, not just the latency.
- **Server shape (plan 033, 2026-09-09):** `nn_server.py` is a *single-threaded* `selectors` loop with a depth-2 GPU pipeline (launch batch N+1 while batch N's result is read back) — no per-connection threads, no `Queue`. The GPU's own work for a batch of 1–4 is ~300 µs of cuDNN kernels on a 16x9 board and is the floor; everything else was host overhead. Per-request latency at 6 clients went 1.37 → 0.91 ms (idle box), server CPU 77 → 65 %. Two traps if you touch the loop: `epoll` rounds sub-ms sleeps up to 1 ms (use `SelectSelector` + a short spin), and without `cudnn.benchmark=True` the b=1 graph bakes in a 4 ms algorithm. `examples/nn_bench.rs` is the round-trip benchmark (`--server SOCK --threads N`); `train/tests/test_nn_server.py` is the protocol test on CPU.

## inproc (`inproc.rs`, `batch_queue.rs`, `csrc/inproc_shim.cpp`) — the in-process backend (plan 066)

`Backend::InProcess` runs the net **inside the search process through libtorch** — the same library, kernels and CUDA graph capture as the sidecar — behind a batch queue. Behind the cargo feature **`torch`** (off by default: a default build links no libtorch, needs no CUDA headers and builds on a Mac; `botbowl-ui`/`botbowl-worker` forward it as their own `torch` feature). Chosen **process-wide**: `--nn-backend inproc [--nn-device cuda:N]` on every tool that takes `--nn-server` (`NnBackendArgs` in `botbowl-play::cli_args`, flattened next to it) and on `botbowl-worker`, or `BLOOD_NN_BACKEND=inproc` / `BLOOD_NN_DEVICE`. `configure_backend` stores the choice and `from_path_with_server` honours it, so no call site changed; `forward_raw` stays the single dispatch point.

- **The module is the sidecar's.** `python -m bbnn.export_ts NET.pt` (→ `NET.ts`) saves the sidecar's own trace of `BBNet.forward_masked` (`MaskedNet`, batch-2 canvas example, unoptimised, device-free). At load the shim moves it to the device and calls `torch::jit::optimize_for_inference` — the C++ twin of the sidecar's Python call, so BN folding and the conv+add+ReLU fusion happen on the same device through the same code. **The sidecar runs that traced+optimised module, not eager `forward_masked`** (`--jit auto`); matching eager would not have matched the sidecar.
- **The load sequence mirrors `nn_server.main`'s masked path, call for call**: cuDNN flags, load+optimise, the sidecar's two trace-validation forwards (b=1 9x16 board, b=5 full canvas — they advance the TorchScript profiling executor), then per bucket three warm-up calls on a side stream and the capture on a dedicated capture stream into a private pool (`GraphRunner.__init__`), lane 0's twelve buckets then lane 1's. (Not mirrored: the sidecar's eager comparison forwards in the trace check and its unmasked `warm`/`prewarm` at 9x16 — other modules and shapes; tests A/B pass without them.) **Lane and capture streams are the shim's own (`dedicated_stream`), never libtorch's 32-stream pool**: a load draws 27 pool streams, so with pool streams a fifth net's capture stream was the first net's lane-1 stream, and the first net's replays — enqueued from its batcher during the capture — were recorded into the new graph and its queue died. `loading_more_nets_never_disturbs_one_that_serves` pins it. Replay is `GraphRunner.launch` verbatim (one H2D of the pinned staging buffer, replay, D2H of all `bucket` values, D2H of the first `n` policy rows only when asked). Keep `inproc_shim.cpp` in step with `nn_server.py`, or test A fails.
- **The queue** (`batch_queue.rs`, always compiled, CPU-tested): a fixed ring of slots; a search thread stages its request onto the canvas *in its own slot* (the sidecar's staging, now in parallel across search threads), marks it ready and parks (20 µs spin, then `thread::park`); one batcher thread per lane (2 = the sidecar's depth-2 pipeline, `BLOOD_NN_LANES`) takes every ready slot up to 64, copies rows into its bucket's pinned buffer, replays, crops, unparks. **No max-wait timer**; with a batch in flight a free batcher holds until the device is ~250 µs from idle (`JIT`, the sidecar's `JIT_S`). An executor error fails that batch and kills the queue (every later forward fails at once, and callers waiting for a free slot are woken to see it); an owner waiting > 60 s gives up. Either way `NnEvaluator` falls back to tract with one `NN_SERVER_FALLBACK` line (the token the loop already greps). **The registry never evicts**: each loaded net keeps its graphs and pools for the process (gen44, 2 lanes: ~1.0 GB for the first net incl. the CUDA context, ~0.4-0.6 GB per further net on the 1060), so a long-lived process that loads a net per generation runs the device out of memory after a handful.
- **Missing files are errors, never a quiet tract run** (the content-addressed `.pt` lesson): `resolve_torchscript` looks for `<stem>.ts` beside the ONNX, beside its symlink target, then by ONNX content in `BLOOD_NN_TS_DIRS`; nothing found → the load fails naming `bbnn.export_ts`. The hub ships `<stem>.ts` with the ONNX (`ToWorker::Model.torchscript`, protocol v20) and an in-process worker reports a cached net only if its `<id>.ts` is there.
- **The canary stays**: the committed 9x16 fixture goes through the queue (masked canvas path) and must match tract on the caller's ONNX to `< 1e-3`, or the load fails. Registry key: `(device, resolved .ts)`; a second spelling of one net shares the queue but still checks its own canary.
- **GPU memory and the loop (phase 5).** Measured on the 1060 with gen44 (96x8, 13x22 canvas): one process with one net is ~1.0 GB of GPU (≈0.4 GB CUDA/cuDNN context + ≈0.3 GB per lane; 0.7 GB at `BLOOD_NN_LANES=1`), each further net ~0.5 GB. The registry never evicts, so the bound is the process: `train_loop.sh` restarts its local worker every phase (it holds that phase's nets: the generator, plus the drive reference while an overlapped eval runs — 1.56 GB peak), and `scripts/net_check.sh` runs its whole budget ladder in one `override-audit --ladder` process (one context) instead of one process per rung. A long-lived process that loads a new net per generation would grow without bound — restart it, or add eviction.
- Knobs: `BLOOD_NN_CANVAS=HxW` (default: the compiled capacity incl. border, = the loop's `NN_CANVAS`), `BLOOD_NN_LANES` (2), `BLOOD_NN_SLOTS` (256), `BLOOD_NN_DETERMINISTIC=1` (cuDNN deterministic, benchmark off; production is benchmark on, as the sidecar), `BLOOD_NN_INPROC_STATS=secs` (sidecar-style stats line on stderr; `dataset` prints a final `NN_INPROC` line).
- **Tuning knobs (plan 069 item 4; all opt-in, unset = the sidecar's route bit for bit):** `BLOOD_NN_BUCKETS=1,2,…,1024` (the graph buckets; default `BUCKETS`, the sidecar's twelve to 64), `BLOOD_NN_PRECISION=fp32|tf32|fp16|bf16` (`Precision`: fp32/tf32 set the cuDNN *and* cuBLAS TF32 flags — unset keeps torch's defaults, which on Ampere/Hopper means **TF32 convolutions**; fp16/bf16 load the export variant `NET.fp16.ts` from `python -m bbnn.export_ts NET.pt --dtype fp16`, BatchNorm folded in fp32 then cast, fp32 in and out, so queue/staging/shim are unchanged; a missing variant is an error), `BLOOD_NN_CHANNELS_LAST=1` (the `-cl` variant), `BLOOD_NN_OPTIMIZE=0` (skip `optimize_for_inference`), `BLOOD_NN_CANARY_TOL`. A variant file carries its tag as the `bbnn_variant` TorchScript extra file; the load canary's tolerance follows it (1e-3 fp32, 2e-2 fp16/TF32, 1e-1 bf16), so a variant symlinked as some ONNX's `<stem>.ts` loads as itself in a process whose other nets stay fp32 (`hpc/tune/precision_gate.sh` plays fp16 vs fp32 drives that way). `examples/inproc_bench.rs` is the suite's no-search bench (graph replay per bucket, or N clients on the queue); the suite is `hpc/tune/` (README there).
- **Build**: `cargo build --release -p botbowl-ui --features torch` with its own `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`. `build.rs` finds libtorch in `BOTBOWL_TORCH_DIR` or the checkout's `train/.venv` torch wheel (a worktree finds its main checkout's), CUDA headers in `BOTBOWL_CUDA_INCLUDE` or the wheel's `nvidia/*/include` plus `crt/` + `nv/target` from `scripts/fetch_cuda_headers.sh` (nvcc + cccl wheels' headers into `~/.cache/botbowl/cuda_nvcc/`; the torch wheel does not depend on them; `BOTBOWL_CUDA_NVCC_INCLUDE` names such a dir explicitly, which is how the Arrhenius image does it — `CUDA_WHEEL_DIR`/`CUDA_HEADERS_DEST`/`CUDA_ARCH` make the script offline and cross-arch, plan 069, `hpc/arrhenius/README.md`). Binaries get an rpath to the torch and cudart libs (`links = "bbnn_torch"` publishes it; `botbowl-ui/build.rs`, `botbowl-worker/build.rs` apply it) — no `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`.
- Tests: `tests/inproc.rs` (feature `torch`, skips without a GPU) on `fixtures/tiny.ts` / `heads/tiny_heads.ts` (`python -m bbnn.fixture --ts`, built from the committed ONNX): canary, tract agreement, batch invariance under 24 threads, every head knob, missing export and bad device are errors. Bit parity with the sidecar is `scripts/parity066.sh` (tests A/B) and `scripts/gatec066.sh` (test C); see plan 066 for the numbers.

## Parity fixture

`tests/fixtures/tiny.onnx` + `parity_{h}x{w}_*.npy` are **committed** (built by `train/src/bbnn/fixture.py`). `tests/parity.rs` (not `#[ignore]`d) asserts tract == PyTorch to `< 1e-4` at two board sizes — this is what de-risks tract op coverage (Expand/Shape broadcast, ReduceMean, BN fold, dynamic axes). Rebuild the fixture after any `model.py` architecture change: `cd train && uv run python -m bbnn.fixture`. `heads/tiny_heads.onnx` (+ its own `parity_*`) is the plan-063 second case: every non-default head knob (`policy_conv`, `value_pool = "meanmax"`, wider `value_conv`/`value_hidden`), so tract's ReduceMax/Concat and the policy conv's BN fold are covered too; `uv run python -m bbnn.fixture --heads` rebuilds only it (the sidecar canary reads `tiny`'s 9x16 inputs, so leave those alone unless the net itself changed). Any shape the knobs make loads anywhere a 64x6 does — the ONNX names and shapes do not change, and every Python loader rebuilds the net from `BBNet.shape_of(state_dict)`.
